10 — Mentor's Letter (read when you are tempted to skip steps)

Ariful,

You asked me to be the expert and the mentor, so here is the part that does not fit in a table.

1. You already know the answer; the research said it three weeks ago

"Teach QuickBooks and place 200 people" is not a business. "Win clients, train people on those clients' work, hire the best, supply the rest to firms that trust you, and publish what happened" is a business. Hamidur's course and channel do not change that verdict. They make Phase 1 cheaper to start. That is all, and that is useful.

2. The order of operations is the whole strategy

  1. Agency account and the first two paying clients.
  2. Clean the instructor's public footprint.
  3. Brand, site, price, gateway, terms.
  4. Orientation, selection, Batch 1.
  5. Partner-firm agreements, in parallel with 4.
  6. Phase 2 only when the agency has work to give students.
  7. Publish results. Then repeat.

If you swap 1 and 4 because the course is easier to sell than a client is to win, you become the thirteenth QuickBooks course in Dhaka with a nicer logo.

3. Five things to say no to in the first six months

  • A third niche. (One for seats, one for cleanups. That is already two.)
  • Any ad before the landing page exists.
  • Any student who fails the basics test, no matter who refers them.
  • Any client job the reviewer has not seen before it ships.
  • Any public number you cannot show a source for.

4. What each founder must accept

  • Hamidur gives up the "everything tutorial" identity and the free-download traffic. In return he becomes the named, credentialed face of the one honest accounting academy in the market. His CMA, QBO, Xero and NSDA certificates are worth more than 136K views of a LinkedIn trick.
  • Noman accepts that his first year is quality and mentoring, not fame. The reviewer role is the thing that makes the agency safe to buy from.
  • You accept that outbound sales is a scheduled, counted job, 6–9 months before it pays, and that you are the one doing it. Nobody else on the team can.

5. How you will know it is working (or not)

  • By day 90: paying clients exist, Batch 1 has scores, one firm has signed an intake agreement.
  • By month 6: at least 10 of the first 30 students are earning. If fewer, stop marketing Batch 3 and fix the model. Do not "push harder".
  • By month 12: 30–60 people working through you, 8–10 clients, four published cohort reports. That is a real company. 200 a year is a year-3 question.

6. The one sentence to keep

Trained on real books, reviewed by real accountants, results published. Every decision that does not serve that sentence is a distraction.

7. What I need from you to keep going

Answers to file 08. The three that unblock the most: Q-H1 (students so far), Q-H4 (ad spend and registrations), Q-N1/N2 (Noman's profiles and real-file experience). With those, I can turn files 03–07 into the actual selection test, the curriculum v2 outline, the website copy, the first 12 video scripts, the outbound list and sequence, and the partner-firm call script.

03 — Business Model and Phases

Principle (from research file 14, unchanged): client acquisition first, course second. The course is the hiring and selection funnel, not the product. The product is (a) accounting work delivered to foreign clients and (b) trained, pre-screened people supplied to employers.

1. The three engines

ENGINE A  Agency (revenue now)         ENGINE B  Academy (funnel + cash)       ENGINE C  Placement (the promise)
Upwork/Fiverr agency + direct clients  Phase 1 Fundamentals course            Own agency seats
Cleanup, monthly books, UK Xero/VAT,   Phase 2 Marketplace Hunting cohort     Partner firms in Dhaka/Sylhet
e-commerce integrations                Phase 3 Placement track                Local SMEs (Tally + QBO)
Owner: Ariful (sales) + Noman/Hamidur  Owner: Hamidur (teach) + Ariful (mkt)  Owner: Ariful (partners) + Noman (QA)

Each engine feeds the next: agency work gives students real files and reviews; the best students become agency staff; agency case studies and placed students are the marketing for the next batch. That is the "task renewal" loop from the voice note.

2. Roles (proposal; confirm in 06)

Person Owns Does not own

3. Phases of the student journey

Phase 1 — Fundamentals (Hamidur's existing course, upgraded)

Phase 2 — Marketplace Hunting cohort (Ariful's phase; the differentiator)

  • Entry: top 10–15 from Phase 1 by score, plus an interview. Not sold to everyone. Fee Tk 15,000 or free in exchange for a 6-month agency work agreement (student chooses).
  • 6 weeks, hands-on, in small groups:
  • Profiles built with the Noman playbook (positioning, portfolio, catalog, proposal blocks). The material already exists in fiverr/300_noman-quickbooks.
  • Real work from the agency account: each student does supervised tasks on live client files (categorisation, reconciliation, cleanup segments) under Noman's review. This is the thing no competitor can offer without an agency.
  • Daily job triage drill using the scorecard in 07_client-acquisition.md; one proposal a day reviewed by Ariful.
  • LinkedIn + Facebook-group routine from 03_social-media-plan.md.
  • Direct-client sales: how to quote a cleanup, how to convert to monthly.
  • Output: a live profile, 1–3 reviewed real tasks on the portfolio, and a decision: agency staff, freelancer, or job-seeker track.

Phase 3 — Placement track

Three channels, in order of control: 1. Own agency seats. Hire the best as junior bookkeepers on the agency (part-time first, Tk 15–25k/month, moving to Tk 25–40k with client load). Client pays $600–1,000 per seat per month [14, 19]. 2. Partner firms. Supply pre-screened graduates to 10–20 Dhaka/Sylhet offshore firms (list in research file 10). Employer-paid fee: one month's salary or 15–20% of first-year pay [11]. Start the calls in week 1, not after the batch. 3. Local SME jobs.   • Prices are public, on the website, in Taka, VAT handled correctly.
  • Early-bird (fixed, dated) and instalments are the only discounts. No inbox deals, no "last 3 seats" unless it is true.
  • Scholarship: 2 seats per batch for women or candidates from outside Dhaka, by application. Published.
  • Corporate training priced per batch, not per head.

D3. Refunds and promises

  • Full refund within 7 days of the first class, no questions.
  • No job guarantee, ever, in any channel. "Placement support" means: the Phase 2 track, introductions to partner firms, and agency hiring from the top performers. Written in the student terms.
  • Every batch's outcomes published at 90 and 180 days, with the method (how "earning" is defined: at least Tk 10,000 or $100 of paid accounting work in the window).

D4. Quality (agency)

  • Every deliverable is reviewed by Noman or Hamidur before it reaches a client until the staff member has 3 months without a client-raised error.
  • Reconciliation reports attached to every month-end. Written summary in plain English with each delivery.
  • One named account owner per client; response within one business hour in the client's working day.
  • Errors are logged, not hidden; the log is reviewed monthly and feeds the curriculum.

D5. Data security and confidentiality

  • Client files only in the client's own QBO/Xero with role-based user access; no spreadsheets of client data on personal laptops; 2FA on every account; a password manager; no WhatsApp sharing of client documents.
  • Training uses anonymised or synthetic files. A client's file is used for training only with written consent (US clients: IRS §7216 consent language).
  • NDA signed by every student in Phase 2 and every staff member.
  • Offboarding checklist: access removed the same day.

D6. Client policy

  • Contracts for every client (Upwork contract or our own services agreement). Scope, monthly deliverables, deadline (financials by the 10th unless agreed), rate, termination with 30 days' notice.
  • Payment: Upwork for Upwork-originated clients (no off-platform migration; it breaks the rules), Wise/Payoneer/bank for direct clients, monthly in advance for seats.
  • Trial: 2 weeks free on one bounded task for firm clients; a paid sample batch for SMEs.
  • We do not sign tax returns we are not licensed to sign; we prepare and the client's CPA/accountant files.

D7. Partner-firm policy

  • Written intake agreement: what we supply (tested graduates with scores and project files), fee, replacement if the hire leaves within 60 days.
  • We never place a student we would not hire ourselves.
  • We tell students the firm's real pay band before the interview.

D8. Hiring and pay (agency staff)

  • Hire from our own cohorts first. Part-time first (20 h/week) at Tk 12–18k, full-time Tk 20–30k entry, Tk 30–45k at 2 years, team lead Tk 60k+. Benchmarks from research 10.
  • Review at 3 months with the error log. Clear path from Phase 2 student to junior to reviewer.

D9. Content and claims

  • Every number in public content has a source we can show. No invented student numbers, no "42,000 placed".
  • No pirated software, cracks, or "free download" content on any brand or founder channel.
  • Students appear in content only with written permission; earnings screenshots only with permission and context.

D10. Finance

  • Separate accounts for the entity; no founder personal accounts for student fees.
  • Monthly P&L by engine (academy, agency, placement). Yes, in QuickBooks or Xero, so the books themselves are a demo.
  • Six months of personal runway for each founder before quitting any other income (research 17: the first 3 months are near zero).
